# Daily AI and Agent Research - 2026-10-22

## Thesis

Today's strongest signal is that useful agent control points are concrete and early: preserve origin when context is compacted, insert one planning gate before the first durable edit, and evaluate the resulting workflow against users who clarify and change requirements.

## Top findings

### 1. Compaction can launder tool output into apparent user authority

Five flattening coding-agent harnesses preserved forged approvals 87% to 100% of the time, while four role-typed harnesses measured 0%. The immediate fix is structural: preserve typed roles and source lineage through compaction.

Sources: [paper v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29869v1), [repository](https://github.com/pixee/compaction-laundering)

Implementability score: 0.91

### 2. The first source edit is a cheap planning checkpoint

PAUSE places one planner call immediately before the first source-code edit. It reports 14.7% to 16.8% resolution gains in selected online settings and comparable performance to periodic oversight at 11% to 15% of the planner cost.

Source: [paper v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29870v1)

Implementability score: 0.82

### 3. Multi-turn user simulation exposes regressions hidden by static tasks

SWE-DIVE adapts benchmark tasks into conversations with three grounded user personas. Its 11-task pilot found regressions in 4 or 5 multi-turn runs per persona versus 2 for the single-turn baseline, despite similar resolved-task counts.

Sources: [paper v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30047v1), [repository](https://github.com/lorenzbrehme/swe-dive_usersim)

Implementability score: 0.70

## What to implement now

1. Audit the production compactor for role flattening and unescaped in-band labels.
2. Preserve source identity and lineage on every compacted claim.
3. Add a first-source-edit hook that requests one independent repair plan.
4. Add clarification, pushback, and changed-requirement personas to coding-agent acceptance tests.
5. Grade target success and newly introduced regressions separately.
