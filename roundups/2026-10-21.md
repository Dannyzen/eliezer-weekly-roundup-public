# Deep Dive Wednesday - 2026-10-21

## Weekly winner

### CarryoverBench: persistent state is delayed execution authority

CarryoverBench is the week's most important finding because it turns memory and scheduling risk into a stage-conditioned runtime test and applies the Latent channel to Hermes. A one-time indirect prompt injection can be planted into memory or a heartbeat file, survive the original session, and trigger later after the attacker has left.

Across 12 personal-assistant scenarios, 101 attack tasks, and five models, planted payloads showed no monotonic decay over five trigger rounds. Once planted, later execution was frequently the weaker gate. Textual defenses mainly reduced planting and rarely closed the trigger gate.

On 36 Latent variants, Hermes reduced attack success relative to Nanobot, but every tested model remained vulnerable. Reported Hermes attack success ranged from 2.8 percent to 27.8 percent. The result supports a concrete architecture rule: untrusted evidence may persist, but future authority must be re-established at resurfacing and again at effect time.

## Why it won

- TraceLite offered the best immediate efficiency gain, with preserved SWE-bench resolution and lower context cost.
- AgenTest offered the best workflow-quality control, with trajectory-level business-rule tests.
- Proof-Carrying Contracts offered the strongest formal end state for enforceable commitments.
- CarryoverBench combined immediate product risk, direct Hermes relevance, a populated artifact, and a bounded first implementation.

## What is implementable now

1. Attach origin, principal, purpose, scope, trust, expiry, and revocation to persistent writes.
2. Separate facts and preferences from procedures, schedules, and authority grants.
3. Quarantine instructions derived from webpages, email, documents, tool results, and peer agents.
4. Reauthorize scheduled wake-ups and memory-derived effects against current policy.
5. Bind approvals to exact tools, arguments, destinations, and principals.
6. Add isolated plant and trigger fixtures that run in separate sessions and grade real effect receipts.

## Caveats

- CarryoverBench uses synthetic task families and sandboxed tools.
- Latent rounds replay a topically aligned follow-up, so their rates upper-bound mixed real-world sessions.
- The Hermes transfer covers Latent attacks only and excludes one reasoning-model configuration.
- The populated repository currently has no declared license.
- No external source was cloned, installed, built, imported, or executed.
