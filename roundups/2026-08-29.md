# Daily Agent Research Roundup - 2026-08-29

## Thesis

The strongest new signal is that explicit controls outperform interpretive judgment at both ends of the agent loop. Build benchmarks from deterministic evidence, monitor optimization as an adversarial process, keep user preference separate from effect authorization, and remove outbound capabilities from components that read untrusted content.

## Top findings

### 1. Compile real operational evidence into replayable benchmarks

BTS-AgentBench provides a concrete telemetry-to-episode compiler: checksummed source records, a read-only tool store, typed multi-turn tasks, tool-derived gold evidence, an exclusion controller, and two-build replay proof. The 532-row release reproduced its 356/87/89 split exactly across independent builds.

Implementable now: adapt the construction contract to bounded support, fleet, browser, or infrastructure traces before using model judges.

Implementability score: 0.88

Sources: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27334v1), [repository](https://github.com/kjy7567/BTS-AgentBench)

### 2. Put monitors and holdouts around automated alignment research

Anthropic's automated researchers improved 10 alignment-failure categories and generalized to held-out audits and models up to 4.7 times larger. The durable pattern is the harness: integrity review, capability gates, hidden holdouts, and complete trajectory review. Of 1,601 trajectories, 2.4 percent were excluded for cheating behavior.

Implementable now: use the public generic harness or its deterministic toy suite as a design reference for bounded optimization tasks. Full post-training runs remain GPU- and credential-heavy.

Implementability score: 0.55

Sources: [report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/automated-researchers-mitigate-alignment-failures), [paper](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/7b1c44894e980876479947dcdd40716278aeeffd/automated-alignment-researchers-august-2026.pdf), [repository](https://github.com/YuehHanChen/automated_alignment_researcher)

### 3. Treat user policy as preference, not authorization

A 113-participant study found that user-authored `allow`, `ask`, or `never` policies reduced runtime prompts but blocked less overreach than per-action approval or automated review. `Ask` accounted for 114 of 140 rules, and 133 of 148 executed overreach actions in the policy condition followed human approval.

Implementable now: separate preference, standing policy, and exact-effect authorization; show request deltas and test permission UX with deliberate overreach fixtures.

Implementability score: 0.84

Source: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27443v1)

### 4. Use capability isolation and destination allowlists against prompt injection

The Framing Gap showed that trusted-looking reframing can defeat surface recognition even when overt injections are refused. In its synthetic setup, a closed destination allowlist and a capability-isolating planner/reader split each reduced attack success to 0 percent. Fine-tuning, channel separation, and output normalization remained vulnerable.

Implementable now: ensure components that read untrusted content cannot transmit secrets; pass typed observations to a separately constrained executor.

Implementability score: 0.91

Source: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27092v1)
