# Daily Agent Research Roundup: 2026-11-03

## Scope

Today's four findings were submitted November 2 and first listed by arXiv on November 3. GitHub artifacts were inspected read-only. No external source was cloned or executed. NotebookLM remained disabled.

## 1. Structural tests should identify the broken mechanism

Stateful workflows fail through scheduling, candidate selection, state retention, or missing snapshots. A fixed-work diagnostic suite can vary those pressures separately before a team changes models.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03227v1

Implementability score: 0.72

## 2. Rule retention after compaction does not prove compliance

ARCBench finds most post-compaction violations occur while the governing rule remains in context. Store policy outside lossy summaries, test later conflicting requests, and reauthorize actions after compaction.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03223v1

Implementability score: 0.84

## 3. MCP frameworks need preflight and containment, not model trust

Across 9,720 framework-model-attack cells, the tool-poisoning study reports 22.9% attack success and finds no intentional framework-layer defense in the audited systems. Treat all MCP metadata and output as untrusted, then verify real effects with canary-backed sandbox tests.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03555v1

Implementability score: 0.82

## 4. The host must own destructive approval

Approval is frequently absent on both sides of MCP. Host-side prompting for every tool not explicitly trusted as read-only or non-destructive reduces the measured unapproved pairing share from 60.7% to 5.4%. Missing confirmation must fail closed.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02527v1

Implementability score: 0.90

## Daily thesis

Agent safety depends on one explicit effect boundary: summaries may preserve evidence, tools may propose operations, and servers may request confirmation, but the host must recheck current policy and state before dispatch. Evaluation should expose which structural pressure broke that boundary.
