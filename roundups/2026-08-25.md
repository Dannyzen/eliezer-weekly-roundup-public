# Daily AI and Agent Research - 2026-08-25

## Thesis

Self-improvement is useful only when every improvement carries a boundary and proof. Harness patches need held-out trace evidence. Skills need applicability and recovery fields. Runtime edits need exact obligation checks. Benchmarks need to score the declared model-plus-runtime bundle across repeated traces.

## Top findings

### 1. Optimize harnesses through verified patch lineages

AutoSaddler improved base harnesses by 9.0 to 10.0 percentage points across GAIA2, SWE-Bench Pro, and Terminal-Bench 2.0. Its important pattern is a bounded diagnosis, patch, verification, and lineage loop with held-out selection, not unconstrained self-editing.

Implementability score: 0.78

Sources:
- [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23041v1)
- [repository](https://github.com/microsoft/AutoSaddler)

### 2. Make reusable skills boundary-aware

BASM shows that success-only skill memory can increase wrong-tool confidence. Adding applicability, risk, avoidance, and recovery fields improved AppWorld success by up to 23.8 percent and reduced AgentDojo attack success by 4.6 percent.

Implementability score: 0.76

Sources:
- [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22339v1)

### 3. Score the full runtime bundle from traces

ClawProBench evaluates declared model-plus-runtime configurations across 102 live-profile and 68 frozen-holdout scenarios. Strict three-trial pass was 0.2890 versus 0.6638 for pass-at-k-any, and public-profile versus holdout rankings aligned weakly at Spearman 0.13.

Implementability score: 0.88

Sources:
- [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22510v1)
- [repository](https://github.com/suyoumo/ClawProBench)

### 4. Check execution edits before changing agent history

The execution-edit checker treats checkpoint, fork, restore, and merge as operations that must preserve prior authorization, in-flight calls, required results, and policy-compliant completion paths. Lean proofs and executable tests cover six edit forms.

Implementability score: 0.58

Sources:
- [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22928v1)
- [repository](https://github.com/eunomia-bpf/agent-check-restore-safety)

## What is implementable now

- Pilot AutoSaddler's bounded patch-lineage pattern on one owned harness, with hidden validation and human promotion.
- Add applicability, risk, avoidance, and recovery fields to one skill schema and test wrong-tool decoys.
- Adapt ClawProBench's declared configuration, frozen holdout, and strict repeated-pass protocol to one Hermes profile.
- Model checkpoint, fork, restore, and merge as explicit execution edits before enabling them around external effects.

## Watchlist signals

- [Apodex 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23283v1) paired a 35B open-weight model with a public long-horizon workbench and asynchronous agent-team harness. It is worth a later read-only architecture comparison, but its broad performance claims are vendor-authored and were not selected over the more diagnostic findings above.
- [SkillBloat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21929v1) showed token-amplification attacks through trusted skill documents, including one 26.39 times Codex case. It strengthens the case for per-skill budgets and cost telemetry, but its artifact was not publicly linked.
